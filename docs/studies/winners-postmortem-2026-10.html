Why our model missed Netweb, SSWL and Time Technoplast

Three stocks that ran hard while our model ranked them low. We looked at why, then checked whether the same pattern holds across every stock since 2019.

Short answer. Our model is built to avoid bad stocks, not to find winners. One rule even punished Netweb for growing fast. Fast-growing sales on their own turned out not to predict winners. The trait the winners shared that really does predict is a share price that's already rising (momentum), and our model gives it little weight.

The three cases

NETWEB · SMALL

Netweb Technologies

+51% in 6 months from April

In April it ranked 2,158th out of 2,198 small caps, almost dead last.

  • A rule called it a likely fraud. Our "fraud score" (Beneish) treats fast sales growth as a warning sign. Netweb's revenue was growing 88–174% a year, so from April to August it got a −0.2 penalty, enough to drop any stock to the bottom.
  • Without that penalty it would have ranked in the middle (about the 61st percentile), still not near the top, because nothing in our small-cap score rewards growth or a rising price much.
  • This isn't a one-off: 1 in 5 of the fastest-growing companies get flagged by this rule, against about 1 in 30 of companies with middling growth.
SSWL · SMALL

Steel Strips Wheels

+120% in 6 months

From January to April it sat in the bottom half of our ranking. No penalty; we simply didn't see it.

  • The business was speeding up, quarter by quarter. Profit growth went −23% → −2% → 0% → +47%, and operating margin rose four quarters in a row (9.3% → 10.9%). It also announced an acquisition in April and new orders from December.
  • We only look at yearly numbers. A one-off ₹516 cr gain back in March 2024 distorts them, so our system says profit growth is −2.7% while the latest quarter was +47%.
  • The price started rising in June (₹196 → ₹391 by October), before the July results confirmed it.
TIMETECHNO · SMALL

Time Technoplast

+222% in the year from April 2023

The big run was 2023–24. (In the last year it's down 20%: cash flow turned negative, it issued new shares and promoters are selling. So its middle rank today looks fair.)

  • During the run, our score couldn't make up its mind. It jumped between the bottom and the top from one quarter to the next, so a book trading on it would have sold early.
  • The cause: almost half of our small-cap score comes from two fast signals (unusual delivery volume and the reaction to results), and they flip around.
  • What stayed high the whole way: its rising share price, falling debt and growing cash flow.

What the three have in common

Rising share priceAll three had strong momentum. Our small-cap score gives it only 14% of the weight.
Heavily tradedAll three ranked at the very bottom on "delivery %" (1st, 13th, 3rd percentile), which our model counts against a stock.
No "speeding up" signalNothing in the model notices a business accelerating: quarterly growth picking up or margins widening.

Then we tested it on every stock since 2019

Three stories prove nothing, so we rebuilt growth and quality from company accounts for every month from December 2019 and checked which ones actually predicted returns 3–12 months ahead.

One more thing: our current score isn't blind to winners. They do rank above average on it, just not near the top. Only 15% of small-cap winners were in its top tenth.

So what were we missing?

  1. Momentum is under-weighted. It's the most reliable sign of a future winner in our own data.
  2. Fast signals make the score flip-flop, so the book exits winners too early.
  3. The fraud-score penalty punishes growth and has no proof behind it.

Not missing: plain sales growth. It sounds like the answer, but the data says it's already priced in.

Next steps (nothing changes in live picks until approved)

  1. Rebuild the long-hold book on slow signals only (momentum, cheap-and-good, Piotroski) and judge it after costs and tax.
  2. Re-test the fraud-score penalty on history back to 2016, then shrink it or turn it into a "take a look" flag.
  3. Get quarterly results back to 2018 to settle whether widening margins really work.
