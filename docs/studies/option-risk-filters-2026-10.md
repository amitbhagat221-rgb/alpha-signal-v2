# Can any market factor avoid or shrink the option book's worst weeks? (2026-10-22)

**Verdict: no rule is adopted. Nothing passes the pre-registered decision rule once the SENSEX check and the multiple-testing haircut are applied. The nearest thing to a signal is a single measure, "how close the chosen strikes sit in VIX units" (`z_strike`), which flagged all four worst weeks, but it fails on SENSEX and its haircut-adjusted p-value is 0.20.**
- Amit's question was fair: half of the worst four *were* visible. Two (2020-04-28, 2020-05-26) were high-volatility weeks (India VIX 35.3 and 31.5, 97th-98th percentile of entries); a VIX, 20-day-volatility, ATM-IV or "house regime" rule flags both. But those rules only "work" on the 2019–22 window that contains those two weeks, they were *fitted* there, and 2023–26 has no comparable week to test them on (one trade with VIX >= 26.7). Unproven, not refuted.
- The other two worst weeks (2022-07-26, VIX 18.2; 2026-05-08, VIX 16.8) were calm by every volatility measure. Only chain-shape, positioning and calendar measures touched them, and none of those survives a haircut for the 98 tests tried.
- Risk-scaled sizing (lots proportional to 1/VIX or 1/20-day realised vol) is the most honest improvement: it cuts drawdown 21 % in 2023–26 and 41–63 % in 2019–22 at equal average margin, but misses the 30 % bar in the test window, fails the P&L-kept bar in 2019–22 (80 % and 79 % vs 85 %), and fails on SENSEX.
- The walk-forward model on all predictors never flagged any of the four worst trades and does not help (test-window P&L ÷ drawdown 11.8 vs base 13.4).
- Read-only: no DB writes, no change to plan 0022 / 0029, weights, picks or cron. Code `tools/option_risk_filters.py`, tests `tests/test_option_risk_filters.py`. Pre-registration: [option-risk-filters-preregistration-2026-10](option-risk-filters-preregistration-2026-10.md).

## Plain words

- **India VIX.** The market's own forecast of how much NIFTY will move over the next 30 days, quoted as an annualised percentage. 16 is calm, 25+ is stress. It rises when people pay up for protection, which is exactly when we are paid most for selling it.
- **Realised volatility.** How much NIFTY *actually* moved lately (here the 5- and 20-day standard deviation of daily returns, annualised). Implied (VIX) minus realised tells us whether options were rich or cheap compared with what just happened.
- **Put-call ratio (PCR).** Puts open (or traded) divided by calls open (or traded). High means more hedging or bearishness, low means more call buying. Here taken on the nearest expiry, which is only two days away at our entry, so it is noisy.
- **Drawdown.** The biggest fall from a peak of cumulative profit. Here in percent of margin, summed trade by trade (no compounding).
- **Walk-forward.** Fit a model only on the past, trade the next year with it, then refit with that year added. Nothing it sees on the day was unknown on the day.
- **Overfitting.** Choosing a rule because it happened to fit a handful of past events, so it looks wonderful on those events and fails on new ones. Here only four trades lost more than 2 % of margin in eight years, so any rule that "avoids" them is at serious risk.
- **Equal average margin.** A rule that skips trades also deploys less margin, which trivially reduces drawdown. To compare fairly, the filtered book is scaled up so it averages the same margin as the base book; then drawdown and P&L are comparable.

## What was tested

- Trades: the plan 0022 rule on NIFTY, 399 trades 2019-02-12 → 2026-10-15 (exp2, 0.05 delta, strangle), regenerated by the backtest's own functions (they match the `option_premium_backtest --trades` file row for row). SENSEX 143 trades from 2024-01. Per-trade return is net P&L ÷ margin. ₹ figures assume the base book deploys all ₹35 L as margin every week (no lot rounding): base NIFTY earns 31.4 % a year of margin in 2019–22 (₹10.99 L), 17.8 % in 2023–26 (₹6.24 L), worst trade −8.9 % of margin, max drawdown −12.8 % (2019–22) and −5.3 % (2023–26).
- 49 predictor tails (a tail = "high values dangerous" or "low values dangerous"; for variables whose sign is not obvious both tails were run and both are counted) x 2 actions (skip, half size) = **98 tests**, plus risk-scaled sizing (2) and one combined model. Thresholds: 70/80/90/95th percentile of the predictor on 2019-02 → 2022-12, best P&L ÷ max drawdown there, then frozen for 2023-01 → 2026-10, for SENSEX and for the 2015–19 stress model.
- **Point-in-time.** Every value is the one known at the entry close: NIFTY/VIX closes of the entry day; `fno_iv_history` and `fno_pcr_history` for the entry day (they are built from the same end-of-day chain the trade is priced on); US 10-year, USDINR, Brent and FII participant positioning taken from the last observation *strictly before* the entry date (the US close, and the NSE participant file, print after India shuts). Events (RBI, Budget, election results, FOMC) are scheduled and public in advance; the list is the one in `option_premium_backtest.py`. `tests/test_option_risk_filters.py` checks that truncating all inputs at the entry date does not change the row, and that perturbing the future (and same-day macro/FII) does not either.
- **House regime (`scoring/regime.py`).** Reconstructable: it is a pure function of the VIX path (band + 3-day hysteresis), so it was replayed on `vix_history`. It is a VIX threshold rule in disguise (CAUTION = VIX >= 25 for 3 days), not an independent signal.

## The one rule that touched all four: `z_strike`

`z_strike` = how far the sold strikes sit from the forward, measured in VIX-standard-deviations of the hold (ln(K/F) ÷ (VIX x sqrt(days/365)), averaged for call and put). Because the strikes are always the 0.05-delta ones, a *low* value means the two-day option implied volatility was *below* the 30-day VIX: the front of the curve was pricing the next days as calmer than the month. All four worst trades sit in the lowest 36 % of entries (percentiles 26, 32, 36, 13). The rule "act when z_strike is in the lowest ~30 % of 2019–22 (threshold 1.83)" flags 39 % of all trades.
- Chance a random 39 % of trades contains all four worst: 2.3 % (uncorrected, descriptive, full sample).
- Test window: half size has permutation p = 0.002, Bonferroni x98 = **0.20**; skip p = 0.11. Benjamini-Yekutieli adjusted p = 1.0. Not significant after the haircut, and the t-equivalent bar for 98 tests is |t| >= 3.5.
- SENSEX: P&L ÷ DD 5.7 (half) / 4.4 (skip) vs base 7.1; drawdown +22 % / +55 % worse. It does not travel.
- Gives up 18 % (half) to 36 % (skip) of test-window P&L. A plausible reading (front-end implied vol cheap against the month, so sold options are underpriced for the move that follows) is a post-hoc story; it was not a hypothesis before the search.
- Status: **a lead to watch forward, not a rule.** Log it in the paper record and re-read after 25+ more weeks; do not size on it.

## The combined model

Walk-forward logistic regression (L2, C = 0.3) on 31 standardised predictors with at least 90 % coverage (FII positioning excluded for coverage), refit every January on trades that expired before it (expanding), target = the bottom 5 % of training returns, action = half size when the predicted probability is in the top decile of the training fit. First predicted year 2021.
- 2021–26: 59 of 300 trades halved, annual P&L 18.6 % (base 20.4 %), max DD −5.3 % unchanged, P&L ÷ DD 21.0 vs base 23.0.
- 2023–26 test: 50 of 196 halved, annual P&L 15.7 % (base 17.8 %, ₹5.50 L vs ₹6.24 L), max DD unchanged at −5.3 %, P&L ÷ DD 11.8 vs 13.4, permutation p = 0.31.
- SENSEX 2024–26 (NIFTY-trained yearly models applied): 30 of 143 halved, P&L ÷ DD 6.4 vs 7.1, drawdown unchanged.
- It flagged none of the four worst trades. The 2020 weeks had no precedent in its training data (first fit uses 2019–20 expiries), and the 2022 and 2026 weeks look like ordinary weeks to a regression. Coefficients (fit through 2025) are small and some have counter-intuitive signs: depth below the 52-week high (+0.52) and "CAUTION regime" (+0.47) push towards "bad", while absolute 1-day return (−0.62) and `z_strike` (−0.61) push towards "bad" when low or small.
- Fails all three conditions (1 and 2 on the test window, SENSEX as well).

## SENSEX and stress-model checks

- **SENSEX** (143 trades, 2024-01 →): the 8 rules passing the NIFTY test window all fail here (drawdown 17–81 % worse, P&L ÷ DD below base 7.1). The VIX / ATM-IV / regime rules flag almost nothing there (VIX never high), so they do not change SENSEX. No rule passes conditions 1-3 on SENSEX.
- **Stress model** (2015-06 → 2019-02, NIFTY + VIX only, Tuesday entries, model calibrated on the real-chain trades; 177 entries, base DD −28.4 % of margin, base 8.9 % a year): the volatility-level rules never trigger (VIX under the thresholds), but backward-looking move rules do: skip when the 20-day return is <= −1 % takes drawdown from −28.4 % to −1.0 % and lifts P&L (10.2 → 13.5 % a year); skipping when NIFTY is 9 % or more below its 52-week high cuts DD to −16.3 %; skip after a 1-day move >= 1 % cuts to −17.4 %. These were chosen on 2019–22 and *fail* the NIFTY chain test (20d-return rule: 2023–26 P&L ÷ DD 7.4 vs 13.4, 45 % of P&L given up). The stress model is synthetic (a calibrated formula, not real option prices) and its −28 % drawdown comes from the 2015–16 falling market; it is a third opinion, not evidence.

## Multiple testing

- Tried: 49 predictor tails x 2 actions = **98 rules**, + 2 risk-scaled sizings + 1 combined model = 101 looks (the 98 are what the haircut is applied to; the other three are fixed-form and fail anyway).
- Statistic: for each rule, the test-window P&L ÷ max drawdown of the filtered book against 20,000 random flaggings of the same number of trades (same sizes). One-sided permutation p-value. Then Bonferroni (x98; implied two-sided |t| bar at 5 % = 3.48) and Benjamini-Yekutieli (the headline correction in `tools/multiple_testing.py`; its `_bh_adjusted` is called directly, because that tool's own input is factor IC t-stats).
- Result: smallest raw p = 0.002 (`z_strike(low)` half); Bonferroni 0.196; BY 1.0 for all 98. **No rule survives.** 8 of 98 pass the raw test-window conditions, and all 8 hinge on that single trade.
- Power: only 4 trades lost more than 2 % of margin, and one of them is the entire test-window drawdown. A filter cannot be proven on four events, which is what the pre-registration said.

## What would and wouldn't have saved us

- **2020-04-28 and 2020-05-26 (VIX 35 and 31, NIFTY +5 % through the call).** A VIX >= 26.7 skip or half-size, or the house CAUTION/CRISIS regime, flags both. That is real, and it is the closest thing to a "factor that predicted the worst drawdown". Caveats: the threshold was learned from a window that contains those weeks; the rule cost about 27 % of 2019–22 P&L and skipped weeks that on average paid well (22 trades, +35 % of margin); and nothing in 2023–26 can confirm it. Sizing by 1/VIX would have cut these trades to roughly half of normal size without any fitted threshold (2019–22 worst trade −8.9 % → −4.7 %), at the price of smaller positions in calm weeks. A *prospective* crash-week would test it; a 2019–22 backtest cannot.
- **2022-07-26 (VIX 18, +2.6 % with an FOMC hike inside the hold).** Volatility rules do not flag it. The FOMC flag does (and 2020-04's FOMC), but FOMC is inside 12 % of trades, gives up 6–11 % of test P&L and has permutation p = 0.62. 52-week depth (>= 9 % below the high, 80th pct) also flags it, but flags 20 % of trades and cuts 28 % of test P&L.
- **2026-05-08 (VIX 16.8, −3.5 % through the put).** No volatility measure, no scheduled event, no stress in rates/rupee/oil. Only chain shape (`z_strike`), the 1.05 put-call volume ratio and FII's heavy net-short futures position (−0.72) were unusual, each only for this single event, and none survives the haircut. Nothing in the data would have predicted this week with confidence; it is a normal-vol gap week, which is exactly what a 0.05-delta strangle is exposed to.
- **The honest framing.** Two of the four worst weeks were visible as high-volatility weeks and are *partially* addressable by sizing down when VIX is high; the other two were not visible. A rule that would have saved 2020 is, at best, insurance bought after the fact, with a cost we can measure and a benefit we cannot test. The drawdown budget (plan 0029 sizing by worst-case loss, not by margin) is still the only protection that does not depend on prediction.

## If a rule had passed

None did, so nothing in plan 0022 or 0029 changes. For the forward test (no code change made here), the cheapest thing that would let a future review settle `z_strike` and the VIX insurance is to keep logging, at every paper entry, the predictor values this tool uses (VIX, 20-day realised vol, `z_strike`, ATM IV) next to the trade; the paper record already holds the strikes, the credit and the VIX. After 25 more weeks, and especially after the next VIX > 25 entry, re-run `python -m tools.option_risk_filters`, which is read-only and reproducible.

## What would falsify what I found

- The VIX insurance would be falsified by a high-VIX (>= 27) entry that wins big in the next run of weeks (the cost of skipping/halving is already measured: about 18 % of all-period P&L), or confirmed by a loss from a VIX > 27 entry that sizing down would have cut. Neither has happened since 2022.
- `z_strike` would be falsified by the next 25+ weeks in which its flagged half does not lose more than the unflagged half, per the same permutation test.
- Any filter would need to flag most of the next worst week *and* survive SENSEX; the SENSEX drawdowns got worse for every positioning-type rule.
