01

The tea-stall version

What does this industry actually do, in one breath?

You never buy from these companies. You buy the paint, the medicine, the pesticide, the fridge, the shirt. They sell to the people who make those. There are three kinds of chemicals business inside the 318 names, and they behave like three different sectors. The first question for any name is: which door is it behind?

1 · Commodity

Sold by the tonne. Price set by the world. The company is a price taker.
  • Caustic soda, soda ash, PVC, phenol, methanol
  • Gujarat Alkalies, DCM Shriram, Deepak Nitrite's phenol
profit the company keepslow

2 · Specialty

Sold for what it does, not what it weighs. Two or three makers per molecule.
  • Refrigerant gases, crop-protection ingredients, pigments, flavours
  • SRF, Navin Fluorine, Vinati, Atul, Aarti
profit the company keepsmedium

3 · Contract manufacturing

You make a Western company's patented molecule for them, for years.
  • CSM / CDMO contracts, multi-year, slow to win and slow to lose
  • PI Industries, Navin's CDMO arm, Anupam Rasayan
profit the company keepshigh
Analogy. Door 1 sells rice by the sack at mandi price. Door 2 sells a spice mix people ask for by name. Door 3 is a chef cooking another restaurant's secret recipe under a five-year contract.
So what for the stockA commodity name is a bet on a price. A specialty name is a bet on volume in a niche. A contract name is a bet on contracts, and it falls hardest when one is lost.

How a rupee becomes profit

05

The clock

Is it cyclical, what does one full cycle look like, and what time is it now?

Yes, cyclical. Commodity chemicals move with price. Specialty moves with customer inventories. On top of both sits a slow structural tailwind: the world's wish to buy less from China.

2019China shuts polluting plants. Indian prices firm up. The China+1 story is born.
2020–22Covid snarls supply, China rations factory power, then the 2022 Europe gas crisis makes European plants uneconomic. Specialty EBITDA margins touch the high twenties. Stocks re-rate to P/E in the forties and fifties.
2022The peak. Everyone orders too much for fear of shortages. Everyone announces capex.
2023–24Customers find their warehouses full and stop ordering. Chinese capacity built in the boom comes online and exports at any price. Product prices fall by a third to a half. Indian gross margins shrink 5 to 10 points. Stocks halve.
2025The bottom. Destocking ends, volumes return, prices do not. India extends anti-dumping duties. Europe closes capacity for good.
2026Early recovery, volume-led not price-led. Phthalic anhydride duty extended, HFC duty extension recommended, five new anti-dumping probes, Bhavya Rasayan scheme and three chemical parks announced.

Four hands to read the clock yourself. In the live page each hand is a computed value with its last reading.

1 · Chinese chemical export prices flat
Rising means the cycle is turning up for real
2 · Indian gross margins, q/q ▲ expanding

Porter's five forces

ForceWhy that score
RivalryCommodity: the world price, set by China. Specialty: two or three makers per molecule.
Supplier powerFew feedstock sellers (Reliance, IOC, GAIL); China controls fluorspar.
Buyer powerCommodity buyers switch on price. Contract buyers take years to qualify a plant and sign for years.

So what for the stockCommodity keeps little of the profit, specialty keeps some, contract keeps the most. That is why the market pays the most for contract names and why a lost contract is the worst single headline in this sector.

Tell a friend

The 60-second version you can repeat without notes.
≈ 60 seconds aloud
Chemical companies buy messy stuff from refineries and mines, cook it, and sell cleaner stuff to the people who make paint, medicine, pesticide and plastic. Their profit is one gap: selling price minus raw material. China sets the selling price for most of the world, so when China is weak and exporting cheap, Indian margins get squeezed, and when China cuts supply or Europe's gas gets expensive, Indian margins fatten. From 2020 to 2022 that gap was huge and the stocks went crazy. In 2023 and 2024 the gap collapsed and the stocks halved. Now volumes are back but prices are not, so it is early recovery. The companies that do best own a niche molecule or make someone else's secret recipe under a long contract. The ones that do worst sell by the tonne. Look at gross margin and new-plant asset turns, and watch Chinese export prices and the European gas price.

Glossary

Every term used above, in one line each.
Spread
Selling price minus raw material cost. The sector's one big number.
Gross margin
Spread as a share of sales. Sales minus raw materials, divided by sales.
EBITDA
Earnings before interest, tax, depreciation and amortisation. Profit from running the plant, before paying for the plant or the loan.
Depreciation
The yearly charge that spreads a plant's cost over its life. It starts the day the plant opens, whether or not it is full.
Capex
Capital expenditure: money spent building or expanding plants.
Capital work in progress (CWIP)
Plants under construction, not yet depreciating. Tomorrow's capacity.
Asset turn
Sales per rupee of plant. 1.2 means ₹1.20 of yearly sales for every ₹1 of plant.
Working capital
Money tied up in inventory and unpaid customer bills, minus unpaid supplier bills.
Inventory days
How many days of sales sit in stock. Rising means customers have stopped buying.
Destocking
Customers running down their own stock instead of ordering. Orders fall even if end demand does not.
ROCE
Return on capital employed: operating profit divided by the money invested in the business.
Price taker
A seller who must accept the world price because its product is identical to everyone else's.
CSM / CDMO
Custom synthesis manufacturing, or contract development and manufacturing: making another company's molecule under contract.
Chlor-alkali, ECU
Splitting salt water with electricity into caustic soda and chlorine. ECU is the price of one unit of both together. Power is half the cost.
Fluorspar
The mineral all fluorine chemistry starts from. China mines most of it.
Anti-dumping duty, DGTR
A tariff on imports sold below fair price. DGTR is the Indian body that investigates and recommends it.
BIS quality control order (QCO)
A rule that an imported chemical must meet an Indian standard before it can be sold, which blocks cheap imports.
Kigali, HFC
The global treaty phasing down HFC refrigerant gases. Each country gets a shrinking quota; India's freeze starts 2028.
REACH
The EU's chemical registration rule. Every substance sold in Europe must be registered with safety data.
PFAS
"Forever chemicals", a family that includes many fluoropolymers. The EU is considering broad limits.
Carbon border tax (CBAM)
An EU charge on the carbon in imported goods. Covers steel, cement, aluminium, fertiliser, electricity and hydrogen today.
TTF
The Dutch gas price that Europe's chemical plants pay. High TTF makes Europe uneconomic.
PPI
Producer price index: the price factories sell at. China's chemical PPI is the world's price ceiling.
China+1
Buyers adding a second supplier outside China, often in India.
Model score, breadth
Our ranking: the market-cap weighted average score of the industry's stocks, and the share of its ranked stocks scoring 55 or more.
