Research 0007 + winners post-mortem · plain-language summary · 13 Oct 2026

Building a slow stock book: what we know and what we'll do

We want a stock book we can hold for 3–12 months instead of 5 days. This page brings together three pieces of work: our own test of 48 slower rules, a look at three winners our model missed, and three rounds of outside research. It ends with one set of recommendations.

The answer in five lines

  1. For real money today, use index funds in every size group. None of our rules has proven it beats the index yet.
  2. Our model is built to avoid bad stocks, not to find winners. It gives little weight to the most reliable sign of a future winner (a share price that's already rising), and one rule even punishes fast growers.
  3. We know how to run a slow book: buy strictly, sell lazily, use only slow signals, and treat bad news as a reason to look again, never as an automatic sell.
  4. There's nothing proven to copy. NSE's factor indices lost their back-tested edge once live, and no famous Indian investing style has an independently checked record.
  5. The next step is a better slow score, tested honestly for luck. Nothing changes in live picks until that test passes and Amit approves it.

Background

The problem: our book trades too much

Today's paper book is rebuilt every day: buy a stock when it enters the top 5 of its size group, sell it when it falls below 8th. A stock is typically held for only 5 trading days, and about 9% of the book changes every day. Every trade costs money (brokerage, STT, the bid-ask gap), and gains held under 12 months are taxed at 20% instead of 12.5%.

The model's signals were also chosen for how well they predict the next month. Some of them (like unusual delivery volume) work for a few weeks and then fade, so the ranking keeps changing and the book keeps trading.

Evidence 1 of 3 · our own test (plan 0024)

We tried 48 slower rules. None proved itself.

We kept the same idea (buy high-ranked stocks) but slowed everything down, using a "slow score" built only from signals that last for months. The rule works like this:

Buy
Hold
Sell
top 10rank 11–30below 30 → sell (after the minimum hold)

Buy only stocks in the top 10; once owned, keep them until they fall below rank 30. The gap between "buy" and "sell" stops the book from trading every time a rank wiggles.

The rule has five settings. We tried every sensible combination, which made 48 versions in each size group:

SettingValues triedWhat it controls
How often we reviewmonthly or quarterlyhow often we may trade at all
How many stocks we hold10, 15 or 20the size of the buy zone
When we sellbelow 2×, 3× or 4× that numberthe size of the hold zone

Every version was run on 52 months of data the model had never seen (June 2022 – October 2026), with real Zerodha costs and Indian tax, against the tier's index fund.

  • The mechanics work. The best small-cap rule held stocks for about 13 months, traded about 88% of the book a year (against 300–900% for a fast 5-in/8-out book), and 98% of its gains were taxed at the long-term rate.
  • The edge isn't proven. "t" measures how sure we can be a result isn't luck: about 2 or more usually means real. Ours is 1.81. And we picked the best of 48 tries: ask 48 people to flip a coin ten times and someone gets 8 heads without skill. For "best of 48" our bar was about 3.5.
  • It's fragile. The same small-cap rule on a second, equally reasonable slow score made only +3.6% a year.
Evidence 2 of 3 · winners post-mortem

Three winners our model ranked low, and why

NETWEB · SMALL

Netweb Technologies

+51% 6 months from April

Ranked 2,158th of 2,198 small caps in April. A "fraud score" rule (Beneish) treats fast sales growth as a warning sign; Netweb's revenue was growing 88–174% a year, so it got a −0.2 penalty that sent it to the bottom. Without it, it would have sat mid-table: nothing in our small-cap score rewards growth or a rising price much.

Not a one-off: 1 in 5 of the fastest-growing companies get flagged by this rule, against about 1 in 30 with middling growth.

SSWL · SMALL

Steel Strips Wheels

+120% 6 months

Bottom half of our ranking from January to April, no penalty. The business was speeding up quarter by quarter: profit growth went −23% → −2% → 0% → +47%, and operating margin rose four quarters running (9.3% → 10.9%). We only use yearly numbers, and a one-off ₹516 cr gain in March 2024 makes our system read profit growth as −2.7%.

The price started rising in June (₹196 → ₹391 by October), before the July results confirmed it.

TIMETECHNO · SMALL

Time Technoplast

+222% in the year from April 2023

During its 2023–24 run our score couldn't make up its mind, jumping between the bottom and the top from quarter to quarter. Almost half of the small-cap score comes from two fast signals, and they flip around. A book trading on it would have sold early. What stayed high the whole way: its rising share price.

What three rounds of research found

How to build the bookClear. A gap between buy and sell rules is the best way to cut costs. Move slowly toward the target and trust slow signals more. Selling the biggest recent winners or losers cost funds 0.7–1.5% a year against selling at random (783 portfolios, Journal of Finance 2023).

Why we can't prove much yet

We have 83 monthly snapshots. But a 6-month bet started in January and one started in February share 5 of their 6 months, so they aren't separate results. Counted honestly we have about 13 separate 6-month results, like 13 coin flips.

83 dots = 83 months. Only the 13 coloured ones are separate 6-month results.

The usual statistics are too confident with overlapping results. Picking the best of many rules needs its own luck test. And paper-trading our best rule would take about 5 years to prove it, so a 1–2-year trial would almost surely end "can't tell".

Recommendations

What we recommend

Do now · real money

1. Hold index funds in every size group

Nifty 50 for large, Nifty Midcap 150 for mid, Nifty Smallcap 250 for small. Don't trade the daily 5-in/8-out paper book with real money. If a small side bet is wanted, run it only with the slow rules below, sized so that being wrong doesn't matter.

Do now · book rules

2. Run any stock book with slow rules

  • Review monthly. Buy only the top 10; sell only below rank 30.
  • Rank on the 3-month average score, not this month's.
  • Hold each stock at least 12 months before the sell rule applies; if a sale is due, wait up to 42 days when that turns a short-term gain into a long-term one.
  • Equal amounts in each stock.
Do now · alerts

3. Bad news means "look again", never "sell now"

Rising promoter pledges, a rating cut, an auditor or director resigning, or a surveillance-list entry opens a review at the next monthly check. Sell only if the stock's slow score has also dropped below the hold zone. Never sell just because the price moved a lot.

Test first · model changes

4. Fix what made us miss the winners

  • Build a separate slow-only score: more weight on 6-month momentum, add "cheap and good quality", keep Piotroski, and leave the fast signals (delivery anomaly, results reaction) out of this score. The current fast score is not changed or deleted (see 5).
  • Turn the fraud-score penalty into a review flag unless a test on 2016+ history shows it works. Keep the distress score (Altman), which looks more useful.
  • Re-test the midcap accruals weight, since Indian evidence says it doesn't work.
  • Don't add plain sales-growth factors. Don't build a "steady high ROCE" midcap book.

None of this goes live until it passes the luck tests and Amit approves it.

Test in parallel · second book

5. Keep the fast score for a small-cap tactical book

The fast signals are wrong for a months-long book, but they may be right for a shorter one. Today's live score stays exactly as it is (frozen as "fast v1"). We'll test a small-caps-only tactical book on it, held for weeks to about 3 months. Real small-cap trading costs turned out to be 0.31% a trade, not the 1.5% we had assumed, which makes this worth a fresh look. It must still clear the 20% short-term tax and the same luck tests.

Always · testing discipline

6. Test honestly before believing anything

  • Count separate results (about 13 for 6-month bets), not monthly snapshots.
  • Run the two luck tests before trusting any "best of many" rule.
  • Write down each test's features and pass mark before running it.
  • Expect live results to come in lower than the back-test.
Don't

7. Things not to do

  • Don't paper-trade "the best of the 48 rules" expecting an answer within 1–2 years.
  • Don't copy NSE factor indices or famous investing styles on the strength of their back-tests.
  • Don't expect large caps to beat the index: no signal we tested has a real edge there.
Plan

Next steps, in order

The full to-do list, with files, pass marks and effort, is plan 0025. Steps 1–6 only read data and write reports. Nothing changes in live picks unless Amit approves a change afterwards.

  1. Test whether our best rule is just luck

    What
    Two standard checks on the 48 rules already tried.
    Deflated Sharpe ratio: works out how good the best of 48 would look if none had skill, and asks if ours clears that. It first estimates how many truly different tries we made, since the 48 are very similar.
    Probability of back-test overfitting: cut the 52 months into 6–8 blocks; many times over, pick the best rule on half the blocks and see where it ranks on the other half. If the "winner" often lands in the bottom half, the choice was luck.
    Decides
    Is there anything worth paper-trading, or is the index the answer?
    Effort
    About a day.

  2. Build the slow-only score and rerun the book

    What
    Momentum + cheap-and-good + Piotroski, no fast signals. Rerun the small and mid books after costs and tax, judged with the step-1 checks.
    Decides
    Whether a better score turns "+18% but maybe luck" into something we can trust.
    Effort
    A few days.

  3. Re-test the fraud-score penalty and the accruals weight

    What
    Rebuild the Beneish fraud score and Altman distress score from yearly accounts back to 2016, measure whether flagged stocks really did worse, and re-check the midcap accruals weight the same way.
    Decides
    Keep, shrink, or turn each into a review flag.
    Effort
    A few days.

  4. Test the fast small-cap tactical book

    What
    Using today's live score unchanged: re-run the small-cap "how many names" test at the real 0.31% trading cost and 20% short-term tax, then try up to 24 versions of a tactical book (weekly or monthly review, top 10 or 20, a hold zone of 2× or 3×), judged with the step-1 luck tests. Also check how much it overlaps with the slow book.
    Decides
    Whether a second, faster small-cap book is worth running beside the slow one.
    Effort
    About 4 days, alongside steps 2–3.

  5. Measure warning signs on our own data

    What
    From the BSE announcement history (2018 on, including delisted companies): what happened 3 and 6 months after a rating cut, an auditor or CFO resignation, a surveillance-list entry, or a promoter block sale; and how often each was a false alarm.
    Decides
    Which warnings deserve an alert, and how serious each is.
    Effort
    1–2 weeks.

  6. Try "review, not sell" on the book

    What
    Replay the slow and tactical books with the step-5 alerts: an alert opens a review; the stock is sold only if its slow score has also fallen out of the hold zone.
    Decides
    How many extra trades alerts cause, and whether they save or cost money.
    Effort
    About a week.

  7. Get quarterly results back to 2018

    What
    Our quarterly accounts start only in 2023. Collect them from BSE / NSE filings back to 2018.
    Decides
    Whether "margins widening quarter on quarter" (which would have caught SSWL) is real or a one-period fluke.
    Effort
    2–3 weeks; a new data feed.

  8. Extend price signals back to the 1990s

    What
    Rebuild momentum and other price-only signals from NSE daily files back to the 1990s, including companies that later delisted.
    Decides
    Whether momentum holds over 30 years, not just 7, without the flattering bias of only studying survivors.
    Effort
    2–3 weeks.

Words used on this page

Size group (tier)
Large = top 100 companies by market value, mid = 101–250, small = the rest. We only compare stocks within a group.
Slow signal
Something about a company that predicts its share price months ahead, not just weeks.
Momentum
Stocks that rose over the last 6–12 months tend to keep rising for a while.
t
How sure we are a result isn't luck. About 2 or more usually means real; the bar goes up when many ideas are tried.
Back-test
Running a rule on past data as if we had traded it. Live results almost always come in lower.
Piotroski score
A 0–9 checklist of financial health from the yearly accounts.
ROCE
Return on capital employed: operating profit for every rupee the business uses.
Accruals
The part of reported profit not yet backed by cash.
STT
Securities transaction tax: about 0.1% on every delivery trade; only trading less avoids it.
