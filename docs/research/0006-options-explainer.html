Options in two minutes

An option is a contract on an index (like NIFTY) or a stock. It has a strike price and an expiry date. A call pays if the market finishes above the strike. A put pays if it finishes below. The price you pay or receive for the contract is the premium.

You can be on either side. The buyer pays the premium and can lose only that premium, but can gain a lot. The seller receives the premium, keeps it if nothing happens, and has to post a deposit (margin) because the loss on a big move is open-ended. Every graph below shows profit or loss on the day the contract expires, for one lot of NIFTY (65 units), in rupees, for a range of closing levels.

Words you will meet on this page

At the money / out of the money

A strike right at today's price is "at the money". A call strike above today's price, or a put strike below it, is "out of the money": it pays nothing unless the market moves there.

Implied move

What the option market expects the index to move by, read from the price of an at-the-money call plus put. If that pair costs 1.5% of the index, the market is pricing a 1.5% move.

How far out a strike is

We pick strikes by their rough chance of being reached. A "5% strike" has about a one-in-twenty chance of being in the money at expiry. (The technical name is delta 0.05.)

Weekly and monthly expiry

NIFTY has a contract expiring every Tuesday. Stocks and Bank Nifty only have monthly contracts, expiring on the last Tuesday.

Margin

The deposit the broker holds when you sell options. For the trade this project runs it is about ₹1.72 lakh per lot, plus 2% of the contract value on expiry day.

Lot

Options trade in fixed bundles. NIFTY is 65 units per lot. So 10 points of premium is ₹650.

Implied volatility and India VIX

How much movement option prices are assuming. India VIX is this number for NIFTY. When VIX is high, options are expensive.

Backtest and paper trade

A backtest replays a rule on past prices. A paper trade records what the rule would do today, without money, to see if it still works on days it has never seen.

What this project found so far

Two option studies ran this month on real NSE prices from 2019 to 2026. They are the starting point for everything below.

The one trade that works on paper
24.6% a year

On the margin deposit, after modelled costs. Sell a far-out call and a far-out put on NIFTY two days before the Tuesday expiry, every week. 378 trades, no losing year, worst single week −8.9% of margin. It is now on a live paper test.

Safety filters
All lost money

Skipping weeks with high VIX, scheduled events, rich or cheap option prices, a falling market, a scary term structure or crowded positioning all made the result worse, not better.

Stock-level signals
Mid caps only

Among option-based signals for picking stocks, one survives strict testing: the price of downside protection on mid-cap stocks. Large caps show no usable signal at all.

The trade that works: a short strangle

You sell a call far above the market and a put far below it. If NIFTY stays between the two strikes until Tuesday, you keep both premiums. The example uses the project's actual first paper trade, entered on 9 October 2026.

Why the safety filters failed

The natural idea is to skip the strangle, or sell half, when the market looks dangerous. Six such rules were fixed in advance and tested on 2019 to 2024, then checked once on 2024 to 2026. Each bar is how much the rule changed the weekly result, in percent of margin per trade.

Three trade ideas to study next

Each one is written so a program could check its conditions every evening from stored data. Only the first rests on evidence. The other two are ideas to test, with starting values that will be adjusted by the tests, not by opinion.

Idea 1 · The strangle with a safety cap (an iron condor)

Naked version proven on paperCapped version is untested

What it is. The same short strangle, plus you buy an even further-out call and put. Those two extra contracts cost a bit of the premium but put a ceiling on the loss. The shape is called an iron condor.

Why bother. The naked strangle's only weakness is the open-ended loss. A cap also means the broker asks for less margin.

The catch. When the cap was placed close (1% beyond the sold strikes) it failed badly: the whole deposit could be lost on a 1.5% move, and on the eleven big days below it lost its maximum 4 times out of 10. So the open question is whether a much wider cap keeps enough of the premium to be worth it.

Conditions to check each evening.

  • It is exactly two trading days before the Tuesday expiry.
  • The sold strikes are the 5%-chance call and put among strikes that actually traded today.
  • The bought strikes exist on the exchange (on 3 June 2024 there was no listed strike above the sold call).
  • After buying the caps, at least 70% of the premium remains. This number is a starting guess; the test will set it.
  • Today's full option chain is in the database. If not, do nothing.

What we deliberately do not check: VIX, events, trend, positioning. See the filter chart above.

Idea 2 · Buy both sides before a scheduled event (a straddle)

An idea to test

What it is. The day before an RBI rate decision, a Union Budget or an election count, buy the at-the-money call and put. You pay both premiums. If the index moves more than the market expected, in either direction, you profit. Sell at the close on the event day.

Why it might work. On the eleven big days below, the move the market priced in was smaller than the actual move 7 times out of 11, and the straddle was profitable at the event close 9 times out of 11.

The catch. Those eleven days were chosen because the market moved. On a normal RBI day the straddle may just bleed. Nobody has published an Indian study of this, so the project has to run one over all 60 scheduled events since 2019.

Conditions to check each evening.

  • Tomorrow is a scheduled event from a stored calendar (RBI, Budget, election count).
  • The move the market is pricing is cheap compared with past events of the same type (below its median, as a starting guess).
  • Past events of that type moved at least as much as the market priced in.
  • Both contracts traded today.

Most you can lose: what you paid. In the replays that was ₹19,800 to ₹43,600 per lot.

Idea 3 · A one-way bet on a mid-cap stock, with the loss capped (a bull call spread)

An idea to testMid caps only

What it is. When the project's stock model ranks a mid-cap stock in its top tenth, buy its at-the-money call and sell a call 2% higher. The sold call pays for part of the bought one. Profit is capped at the 2% gap; loss is capped at what you paid.

Why mid caps. That is the only size band where the project's stock signals pass strict testing. Large caps have shown no usable edge, so no trade idea rests on them.

The catch. Stock options on NSE are thin, so the cost of getting in and out is unknown. Stock options also settle by physical delivery, so the position must be closed at least two days before expiry. And the stock can be put into the exchange's "ban list" when positions get too crowded, which blocks new trades.

Conditions to check each evening.

  • The stock is mid cap today and has listed options that traded, with open positions in both strikes.
  • It is not in the ban list and not close to it.
  • The stock model ranks it in the top tenth of mid caps, and the option market agrees (downside protection on it is relatively expensive, which in this project's data has gone with higher returns).
  • There are 7 to 25 trading days to the monthly expiry; exit two days before.
  • No quarterly results inside the holding period.
  • The premium paid is at most half the gap between the strikes.

Why there is no fourth idea about quarterly results. It needs a direction first: should we buy or sell the straddle into a results announcement? US studies say buying earns +3.3% before costs and loses 11.8% after costs. The project's own stock option data covers only 27 months. So the first job is to measure every results day since July 2024 and let the sign decide.

What happened on eleven big days

The project replayed eleven famous days from its own stored prices: what the market expected the evening before, and what the three shapes above then did on the weekly contract. One lot, before costs. These days were picked because something happened, so they are the stress test, not the average day.

Was there a warning the evening before?

  • India VIX was in its top sixth on the evening before all six of the biggest moves. The one big move with a calm VIX was the April 2025 tariff shock. But when VIX is high, the straddle costs more, so whether this helps after costs is untested.
  • Foreign investors' positioning said nothing useful. They were net short before the −5.9% election day and also before two +3.8% rallies.
  • Four of the eleven moves were mostly an opening gap. No rule that acts at the close can escape those.

Indian studies of selling premium
Thin

The only cost-aware academic study (monthly contracts, 2015 to 2025) finds every selling variant loses after costs. We could only read its summary, and it has not been reconciled with this project's positive weekly result.

Popular timing tricks with no evidence behind them: "max pain", "gamma exposure", open-interest walls, raw put-call ratios, and "IV rank". The best controlled tests find nothing, and the project's own versions of these signals fail too.

What to build, in order

Ten items at most, because this project has learned that testing too many ideas produces false winners. Each has a pass mark decided before the test runs.

  1. A calendar of trading days to each expiry, with holidays. Pass: reproduces all 397 past entry days exactly.
  2. The capped strangle test (Idea 1). Three cap widths versus the naked strangle, 2019 to 2024 to learn, 2024 to 2026 to check once. Pass: still profitable, and a worst week and worst run at least 3 points of margin better than the naked version.
  3. A table of scheduled events: RBI, Budget, election counts. Pass: matches the 124 dates already typed into the backtest tool.
  4. Expected move before each event, from stored prices. Pass: computable on 95% of the ~60 events since 2019.
  5. Actual move after each event, and the straddle test (Idea 2). Pass: profitable after costs on all 60 events, and better than buying on 95% of random non-event days.
  6. Ban-list history back to 2015. Pass: matches the exchange's own "no fresh positions" rows.
  7. Lot sizes for every stock since July 2024.
  8. How tradeable each stock's options are: daily volume, and from now on the gap between buy and sell quotes.
  9. The mid-cap spread test (Idea 3), after 7 and 8 exist. Pass: profitable after measured costs on the period after September 2025, decided once.
  10. Results-day moves for every stock since July 2024: expected versus actual. The sign of that answer decides whether a results-day idea exists at all.

Data to start saving today, because it cannot be recovered later

  • The end-of-day option chain with buy and sell quotes. NSE serves it, nobody archives it. Without it we can never measure the real cost of trading.
  • Kite's margin quote for each idea's position, each entry day. Needs your daily login.
  • The lot-size file and the next-day ban list.

Everything else can be fetched later: stock options back to 2019 (about 0.6 GB), ban lists and positioning data to 2015, India VIX to 2008.

Decisions only you can make

  1. Spend about 0.6 GB of disk to load stock options from 2019 to 2024? Without it Idea 3 sees only one market regime. Disk was at 80% when this was last declined.
  2. Run the capped strangle on paper alongside the naked one, or wait until the naked one has 25 settled trades?
  3. Keep Bank Nifty in scope? It only has monthly contracts now, and the paper book covers NIFTY and SENSEX.
  4. How much capital for options, and how many lots? The study's rule is to use at most a third of the capital as margin, so the worst day costs 12 to 15% of the pot.
  5. Start saving the end-of-day option chain now, before the design is approved? Every day not saved is lost. It is a data job only, no model change.
  6. Treat 3 August 2026 as a break in the data now, or wait for SEBI's decision on the closing auction?
  7. Get the full text of the negative Indian study and reconcile it with our positive result before extending any selling idea?
  8. Bullish bets only in Idea 3, or also bearish ones? The stock model has only ever been tested for buying.
  9. Which events count for Idea 2? RBI gives 48 past dates, Budgets 10, elections 2.
  10. Spend a whole month's testing budget on options, or mix with the stock work?
  11. Which source for the option-chain archive: NSE's public endpoint, which works today without a login, or Kite, which needs you to log in daily?
