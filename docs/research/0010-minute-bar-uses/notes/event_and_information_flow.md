# Minute bars x timestamped events x cross-stock information flow: return-predictive signals for an NSE factor model

Scope: signals that need minute precision around an EVENT (BSE/NSE filing timestamp, news timestamp, bulk/block deal day) or ACROSS STOCKS (lead-lag, delay, comovement), which daily data cannot measure. Already-listed menu items (realized skew/kurt, signed jump variation, intraday/overnight momentum split, tug-of-war, late-volume share, semibetas, BVC imbalance, closing pressure, VWAP deviation, overnight sentiment, cost model, circuit detection, capacity, HKS periodicity) are excluded; where a candidate below is adjacent to one, the overlap is flagged.

Research budget note: ~30 tool calls; several key PDFs (Jiang-Li-Wang JFE 2021 full text, the 2026 "warp speed" arXiv paper) could not be read past their abstracts. Figures below come only from what was actually read; anything else is in Gaps.

## Q1. Earnings / announcement reactions at minute precision (leakage, speed, in-session vs after-hours, reaction vs drift)

### Takeaway
The strongest published, recent, minute-level evidence is that news-driven returns (identified by matching high-frequency price jumps to timestamped news) keep drifting for several days without reversal, while non-news moves reverse (Jiang, Li & Wang, JFE 2021; US). The two moderating facts for us: US PEAD has disappeared for large caps since 2006 (Martineau, CFR), so any drift edge should be sought in SMALL; and in-session announcements get a ~50% smaller immediate reaction than out-of-session ones (Michaely, Rubin & Vedrashko, RF 2014; US), which matters in India because Indian firms do file results during market hours.

### Cited Findings
- Jiang, Li & Wang, "Pervasive underreaction: Evidence from high-frequency data", Journal of Financial Economics 141 (2021) 573-599: they split daily returns into news-driven and non-news parts using high-frequency data. Prices drift in the same direction as the initial response for several days after firm news arrives, without reversals. A strategy trading the drift earns high abnormal returns and stays profitable after transaction costs. The drift is larger when investors are distracted, and analysts update slowly. US data. — [SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614); [IDEAS/JFE listing](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i2p573-599.html)
- The same authors' working paper "News Momentum" reports that non-news returns are followed by reversal, while news-driven returns continue without later reversal; the post-news drift is persistent and does not reverse with event windows of up to one year (daily decomposition, Dow Jones Newswire). — [Search snippet pointing to SSRN abstract](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614) (working paper; full text not read)
- Martineau, "Rest in Peace Post-Earnings Announcement Drift" (Critical Finance Review): "in modern financial markets, stock prices fully reflect earnings surprises on the announcement date"; "For large stocks, PEAD have been non-existent since 2006 but has only disappeared recently for microcap stocks." Sample figures in the PDF cover 1984-2019. US. — [CFR PDF](https://cfr.ivo-welch.org/published/papers/martineau2021rest.pdf)
- Michaely, Rubin & Vedrashko, "Corporate Governance and the Timing of Earnings Announcements", Review of Finance 18(6) (2014) 2003-2044: with time-stamped newswire data, "earnings news announced within trading hours results in approximately 50% smaller immediate reaction" than comparable out-of-hours news; negative news is more often released during trading hours; after governance regulation tightened, firms moved toward out-of-hours releases, especially well-governed firms. US. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/revfin/v18y2014i6p2003-2044..html)
- India: Prasad & Prabhu, "Does earnings surprise determine the timing of the earnings announcement? Evidence from earnings announcements of Indian companies", Asian Journal of Accounting Research 5(1) (2020) 119-134. Twelve quarterly announcements of the 30 Sensex firms, April 2015 to March 2018. Of the 30 firms, three had a policy of announcing after hours and two during hours. Market responses to announcements during vs after trading hours differ significantly; the market responded negatively to after-hours announcements; a logistic regression finds significant earnings surprises make after-hours announcement more likely. The paper notes SEBI imposes no rule on time-of-day for results. — [Emerald PDF](https://www.emerald.com/insight/content/doi/10.1108/AJAR-04-2019-0023/full/pdf?title=does-earnings-surprise-determine-the-timing-of-the-earnings-announcement-evidence-from-earnings-announcements-of-indian-companies) (daily event study, tiny large-cap sample, not minute-level)
- Grégoire & Martineau, "How Is Earnings News Transmitted to Stock Prices?", Journal of Accounting Research (2022) pp. 261-297: after-hours prices absorb earnings surprises mainly through quote revisions rather than trades, and trade-price returns understate the speed and size of price moves. A 2026 paper describes it as aggregating price changes into one-minute intervals and concluding spreads are too wide for investors to cover round-trip costs. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3060094); [JAR listing](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/412978); [arXiv 2601.08962 (description of precedent)](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08962)
- "Warp speed price moves: Jumps after earnings announcements" (arXiv 2601.08962, 2026) reportedly reaches a different conclusion from Grégoire-Martineau on tradability using a sample of liquid firms. Full text not read (PDF over fetch limit); treat as an unread working paper. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.08962)
- Older US/European speed evidence: initial price reaction appears within the first pair of price changes after a release (a few minutes at most), from a Stanford GSB study on earnings/dividend announcements; a Euronext Paris study reports abnormal returns dissipate within 15 minutes; a Spanish study finds liquidity improvement after in-session announcements appears after about 1.5 hours. Pre-2010. — [Stanford GSB](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/intraday-speed-adjustment-stock-prices-earnings-dividend-announcements) (other two via the same search; individual papers not opened)
- Pre-announcement leakage, India (daily, not minute): Indian buybacks 2006-2024 show significant abnormal returns in the pre-announcement window consistent with leakage, and FII ownership significantly reduces them ([Finance Research Letters, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1544612325018483)); BSE 500 dividend announcements show significantly positive pre-announcement abnormal returns and post-announcement profit booking ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0275531916302781)); Indian mergers show pre-announcement run-ups and unusually high volume before the announcement ([Business Perspectives](https://www.businessperspectives.org/index.php/journals?controller=pdfview&task=download&item_id=1416)).
- Outside directors' purchases before dividend declarations earn positive abnormal returns (non-India), consistent with trading on board-meeting information. — [Review of Accounting Studies, Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11142-023-09774-9)
- Liu (AEA 2024): when ex-ante earnings-announcement risk premia are higher, the market reacts more slowly to unexpected earnings, offered as an explanation of PEAD. Conference paper. — [AEA](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2024/program/paper/5GGEki7i)

### Inferences
- India is unusually well suited to the in-session vs after-hours split: US firms almost always announce outside hours (Prasad & Prabhu say so explicitly), so the US literature has little in-session variation, while our BSE timestamps (2018+) give both kinds in volume. Michaely et al.'s 50% smaller immediate in-session reaction implies more of the information is left to be absorbed later, so in-session announcements should carry more drift. This is a hypothesis to test, not a finding.
- Our daily `announcement_car` very likely mis-dates part of its events: a 10:30 filing's day-0 close-to-close return includes pre-filing minutes; a 15:45 filing's reaction falls on day +1. Minute bars plus timestamps let the window start at the filing minute. This is a data-quality upgrade to a factor that already works in SMALL, and probably the cheapest win in this note.
- Because we only have trade closes (no quotes), Grégoire-Martineau's warning applies twice: we will understate reaction speed, and the "immediate reaction" in thin SMALL names will include bid-ask bounce. Use 5- to 15-minute windows, not 1-minute.
- Martineau's large-cap result argues for testing drift signals in SMALL/MID only, consistent with our in-house finding that announcement_car works in SMALL.

### Gaps
- No minute-level Indian study of results-announcement reactions, pre-filing drift, or reaction completeness was found. NSE working papers / NSE-NYU papers on this were searched for but not found.
- The magnitude of the Jiang-Li-Wang drift (bps/day, horizon in days, Sharpe) could not be extracted; only the abstract was readable (ScienceDirect returned 403).
- No paper was found that directly regresses subsequent drift on the first-N-minutes reaction ("reaction completeness" ratio). That remains our own hypothesis.
- Whether SEBI LODR requires disclosure of board-meeting outcomes within a fixed number of minutes (often said to be 30) was not verified from a primary source.

## Q2. Price delay and information diffusion: intraday Hou-Moskowitz, lead-lag (large to small, sector peers, supply chain, futures to cash)

### Takeaway
Delay and slow within-industry diffusion are well documented with daily/weekly data (Hou-Moskowitz RFS 2005; Hou RFS 2007; Chordia-Sarkar-Subrahmanyam JFQA 2011). The cleanest recent extension uses the day-vs-night split of beta (Yang & Yin, IREF 2024): the long-short earns 0.8%/month raw. At the minute level, cross-stock predictability exists but lives for minutes (Chinco, Clark-Joseph & Ye, JF 2019), so for a daily/weekly model the minute bars are more useful for measuring each stock's delay or follower status than for trading the lead-lag directly. Nifty futures lead cash by minutes, but we have no minute futures data.

### Cited Findings
- Hou & Moskowitz, "Market Frictions, Price Delay, and the Cross-Section of Expected Returns", RFS 18 (2005) 981-1020: the most delayed firms earn a return premium not explained by size, liquidity or microstructure effects; delay is measured from weekly returns regressed on current and four lagged weeks of market returns. US. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v18y2005i3p981-1020.html); [PDF](http://www.ruf.rice.edu/~jgsfss/moskowitz.pdf)
- Yang & Yin, "Stock price delay and the cross-section of expected returns: A story of night and day", International Review of Economics & Finance 96 (2024), 103669: they estimate day beta and night beta, plus an "asynchronous beta" for slow diffusion. The long-short sorted on the day-night beta gap earns 0.8%/month raw and 0.77% alpha; on asynchronous beta, 0.39% raw and 0.28% alpha (one-month holding). The results survive size, B/M, liquidity, investor-recognition and limits-to-arbitrage controls. The premium is stronger in small, value, less visible, illiquid firms, and the measure adds information beyond Hou-Moskowitz delay. TAQ data, so US. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v96y2024ipbs1059056024006610.html); [ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056024006610)
- Hou, "Industry Information Diffusion and the Lead-Lag Effect in Stock Returns", RFS 20(4) (2007) 1113: the lead-lag effect is mostly intra-industry, with large firms leading small firms in the same industry. Cross-industry predictability is weak after controlling for it, and the effect is driven mainly by slow adjustment to negative news. US. — [OUP](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article-abstract/20/4/1113/1615954); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=463005)
- Chordia, Sarkar & Subrahmanyam, "Liquidity Dynamics and Cross-Autocorrelations", JFQA 46 (2011) 709-736: informed trading transmits from large to small stocks with a lag. Large-stock illiquidity from informed trading portends stronger cross-autocorrelation; the lead from large-stock order flow to small-stock returns is stronger when large-stock spreads are higher and before macro announcements. US. — [Cambridge/JFQA](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/abs/liquidity-dynamics-and-crossautocorrelations/E25BA8F3D1B1CA7EEB3416BBA4E53D6F); [NY Fed SR 303](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr303.pdf). Data frequency (intraday vs daily) not confirmed from the abstract.
- Chinco, Clark-Joseph & Ye, "Sparse Signals in the Cross-Section of Returns", JF 74(1) (2019) 449-492: rolling LASSO on the entire cross-section of lagged returns makes one-minute-ahead forecasts; it improves out-of-sample fit and forecast-implied Sharpe ratios. Predictors are unexpected, short-lived and sparse, and the stocks selected tend to have fundamental news. US. — [NBER w23933](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23933.ack); [vLex/JF](https://law-journals-books.vlex.com/vid/sparse-signals-crosssection-returns-855623649)
- Cohen & Frazzini (2008), as summarized secondhand: prices respond slowly to news about economically linked firms (customer-supplier) because of attention limits. US. — [ResearchGate summary](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/2861653_Information_Diffusion_and_Asymmetric_Cross-Autocorrelations_in_Stock_Returns)
- Parsons et al. (geographic lead-lag, working paper): area-level information reaches some firms with a delay. — [AEA 2020](https://www.aeaweb.org/conference/2020/preliminary/paper/SfTyaRaf)
- India, index futures vs cash (index-level): one-minute Nifty 50 data show spot and futures cointegrated, with spot correcting disequilibria and Granger causality running both ways ([Khan 2022, J. Mathematics/Hindawi](https://hindawi.com/journals/jmath/2022/2164974)); another study reports most price discovery in futures, with spot following at a lag of about 9 minutes ([Indian Journal of Finance, high-frequency study](https://www.indianjournaloffinance.co.in/index.php/IJF/article/view/151705)); one study in the same search finds futures strongly led spot until June 2015 and only weakly afterwards (attribution between the two papers above is uncertain from the snippet); NSE research papers note lead-lag can last only minutes under informational efficiency ([NSE comppaper155](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/comppaper155.pdf); [NSE comppaper183](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/research/comppaper183.pdf)).
- An emerging-market study covering South Asia (incl. India) uses Hou-Moskowitz delay as an efficiency proxy and links return predictability to firm size and trading frequency. — [ScienceDirect, "Time-varying return predictability in South Asian equity markets"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1059056016303537)

### Inferences
- An intraday delay measure (5-minute stock returns on current plus lagged 5-minute market returns) will be dominated by stale prices and non-trading in thin names: a minute-level "delay" is mostly an illiquidity measure. The Yang-Yin day/night beta gap is the better-evidenced use of our data, since it needs only overnight returns (daily open plus previous close) and intraday returns, both of which we have. It is distinct from the excluded semibetas and momentum split, but a correlation check against both is required.
- With no supply-chain data, the testable diffusion channel is industry peers (sector map), and the event-anchored version is the most distinctive: when a peer files results at a known minute, measure how much non-announcing peers moved within the next 60 minutes relative to their historical beta to the announcer. Hou 2007 (slow intra-industry diffusion, mainly negative news) is the prior.
- Futures-to-cash lead cannot be tested at the stock level: our F&O data is daily bhavcopy. What we can test is whether F&O-eligible stocks show less minute-level delay than non-F&O stocks, which is a descriptive check, not a signal.
- Chinco et al.'s horizon (1 minute) is useless for a daily/weekly book, but the frequency with which a stock is selected as a predictor of others could serve as an "information leader" score. That is our own idea with no evidence.

### Gaps
- No paper was found estimating intraday (minute) price delay or large-to-small lead-lag for Indian stocks.
- No evidence was found that minute-level delay predicts monthly returns once daily illiquidity is controlled for. This is an open question, and probably the main risk for this family.
- Yang-Yin's sample period and exact construction could not be read (abstract page only).

## Q3. Co-movement from intraday data: realized correlation, comovement after index inclusion, dispersion

### Takeaway
Intraday comovement is well characterized descriptively (US: correlation rises through the session, beta dispersion falls, driven by index-based order flow), and index inclusion raises comovement and beta. No source found shows that a stock's intraday realized correlation predicts its future cross-sectional return. For us this family is a measurement and risk tool (passive-flow exposure, better betas, inclusion effects) more than an alpha source.

### Cited Findings
- Shen & Shi, "Intraday variation in cross-sectional stock comovement and impact of index-based strategies", Journal of Financial Markets 68 (2024) 100894: in S&P 500 stocks, 2011-2021, correlation rises substantially through the trading day while cross-sectional beta dispersion falls, so comovement peaks near the close. They attribute this to intraday shifts between index-based and firm-specific order flow. — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v68y2024ics1386418124000120.html)
- Andersen, Thyrsgaard & Todorov, "Recalcitrant betas", Quantitative Economics (2021): S&P 500 beta dispersion is elevated at the open and declines over the day; the pattern shifts over time and responds to information shocks such as clustered earnings announcements and macro releases. — [Wiley/QE](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.3982/QE1570)
- A 1-second-data study reports that volume and correlation rise at the end of the session, with betas dispersed in the morning and converging toward one during the day. — [arXiv 2310.19992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.19992) (working paper)
- Index inclusion: a comovement model extending Barberis-Shleifer-Wurgler (2005) finds beta changes after additions/deletions at daily and monthly frequency. Low-beta stocks drive most of the post-inclusion beta rise, and arbitrageurs shorting high-beta additions can offset or reverse it. — [ScienceDirect, "Index tracking and beta arbitrage effects in comovement"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1057521922002812). Another source puts the average added stock's beta increase at about 0.10. — [American Academy of Arts & Sciences](https://www.amacad.org/publication/challenges-business-twenty-first-century/section/4)
- Firm-specific news flow moves beta ("Does Beta Move with News?", RFS 2012). — [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/verardom/papers/BetaNews_RFS12.pdf) (abstract-level only)
- Intraday data improves beta forecasts; blending high-frequency with daily/monthly returns approximates long-memory-model accuracy. — [Salt Financial white paper](https://saltfinancial.com/static/uploads/2019/07/Quarterly-Beta-Forecasting-with-Multiple-Return-Frequencies-FINAL.pdf) (industry paper)

### Inferences
- An India-testable measure is `close_comove_gap`: a stock's 5-minute return correlation with the market in the last hour minus the first hour, over a month. Per Shen & Shi it should rise with index/passive ownership (Nifty 50, Next 50, Midcap 150 membership, ETF holdings). Use it as a passive-flow exposure control, or to time the post-inclusion beta rise around NSE index rebalances, not as a stand-alone alpha.
- Note the overlap: "closing pressure" and "late-volume share" are already on the menu. The comovement gap measures correlation, not volume or price pressure, but expect them to be correlated.
- From 3 Aug 2026, CAS covers F&O stocks (see Q5), which removes 15:15-15:30 continuous trading for them, so any last-hour measure breaks at that date for F&O names.

### Gaps
- No paper found linking stock-level intraday realized correlation, or its change, to future cross-sectional returns (US or India).
- No Indian study of intraday comovement or Nifty inclusion effects at intraday frequency was found.
- Cross-sectional dispersion appears only as a market-timing variable in the literature found; nothing cross-sectional.

## Q4. Attention and news: abnormal intraday volume/volatility bursts, reactions to news timestamps

### Takeaway
High abnormal volume predicts higher returns at weekly/monthly horizons (Gervais-Kaniel-Mingelgrin 2001), and a 2025 working paper finds volume shocks predict positive overnight, not intraday, returns. The minute-level contribution is classifying bursts and jumps by whether a timestamped item explains them. In DJIA stocks at least 85% of intraday jumps have no identifiable news, while 96% of overnight jumps do (Aït-Sahalia et al., NBER 2024). Matched news jumps continue, unmatched ones reverse (Jiang-Li-Wang; Xiao 2020 for ETFs).

### Cited Findings
- Aït-Sahalia, Li & Li, "So Many Jumps, So Few News", NBER WP 32746 (July 2024): DJIA constituents, 5-second baseline returns, Thomson Reuters News Analytics. Most relevant news leads quickly to jumps, but at least 85% of intraday jumps lack identifiable public news, while 96% of overnight jumps are explained by news. The share of jumps with news is generally below 15% through the trading day, except in the overnight period and the first and last two minutes of the session, where it exceeds 75% for most companies. Response time declines to zero after 2008. Microstructure variables have only limited power to separate news from no-news jumps. Working paper, US. — [NBER PDF](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32746/w32746.pdf)
- Xiao, "Jumps, News, and Subsequent Return Dynamics: An Intraday Study", Journal of Financial Research (2020): no-news jumps reverse significantly for at least 60 minutes; scheduled-news jumps reverse, unscheduled-news jumps continue; negative jumps reverse more strongly than positive ones. Index ETFs, not single stocks. — [Wiley](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfir.12223)
- A Russell 3000 high-frequency study finds returns tied to news-driven jumps reverse, and that firm-specific news (especially financial-sector) boosts jump spillover. This conflicts with Jiang-Li-Wang's continuation finding. — [ScienceDirect, "News and intraday jumps"](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1062940822000900)
- Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin, "The High Volume Return Premium": stocks with unusually high volume over a day or week, relative to the prior 50 days or 10 weeks, earn higher subsequent returns than unusually low-volume stocks, with attention/visibility as the explanation. US. — [Wharton working-paper version](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf); a JFE paper ties the premium to fundamentals ([ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0304405X20302816))
- Cartea, Cucuringu, Jin & Wilson, "Volume Shocks and Overnight Returns" (SSRN 5156605): volume shocks predict significantly positive subsequent overnight returns, with no such effect in the next intraday session, regardless of market cap; standard risk factors and attention explanations do not account for it. Working paper, US. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/Delivery.cfm/5156605.pdf?abstractid=5156605&mirid=1)
- Lee, Kim & Kim, "Abnormal Trading Volume and the Cross-Section of Stock Returns": the forecasting power of abnormal trading activity is strongly positive up to five weeks ahead. Working paper. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2812010)
- Australian evidence is mixed: the abstract reports a risk-adjusted return above 6% in the year after formation but also "no evidence that volume shocks are priced". — [Pacific-Basin Finance Journal, ScienceDirect](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0927538X16302785) (internally inconsistent snippet; check before use)
- Jiang-Li-Wang (Q1): drift is larger when investors are distracted. — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614)

### Inferences
- The overnight-vs-intraday news-jump asymmetry (96% vs ≤15%) suggests treating the 09:15 opening gap and the first minutes as the "news" segment. For in-session BSE filings, our timestamps can label intraday jumps as news-linked, which the US literature had to do with costly newswire feeds.
- In India, results season concentrates many filings on the same afternoons. A count of concurrent BSE filings in the same 30-minute window is a free, PIT-safe distraction proxy that can moderate any news-drift signal (Jiang-Li-Wang's distraction result is the prior; DellaVigna-Pollet and Hirshleifer et al., cited in Martineau, are the classic distraction papers).
- Minute data adds to the daily high-volume premium by separating bursts with a filing or news timestamp nearby (information) from bursts without one (attention or liquidity). GKM's attention story predicts the premium is larger for no-news bursts. That is a hypothesis, not a finding.

### Gaps
- No Indian study of intraday volume or volatility bursts and subsequent returns was found.
- The news vs no-news continuation/reversal evidence conflicts (Jiang-Li-Wang continuation; the Russell 3000 study reversal; Xiao splits it by scheduled vs unscheduled). How it plays out on NSE is open.
- Our news-item timestamps' latency and accuracy (publication time vs ingestion time) are unknown, which matters for minute matching.

## Q5. Candidate list: formula, horizon, expected sign, evidence, feasibility on close+volume minute bars

### Takeaway
About 14 candidates are computable from close+volume minute bars plus our timestamps. The top three by evidence-times-fit are (1) re-anchoring `announcement_car` at the filing minute, (2) news-matched jump continuation vs unmatched jump reversal, and (3) the day/night beta-gap delay measure. Cross-stock minute lead-lag is better evidenced as a minutes-horizon effect than as a daily/weekly factor.

### Cited Findings
- Data-regime break: from 3 Aug 2026, F&O-eligible stocks get a Closing Auction Session. Continuous trading in them stops at 15:15, the auction runs about 15:15-15:35 (random close between 15:28 and 15:30, matching 15:30-15:35), and the official close becomes the auction price, with a reference price based on the 15:00-15:15 VWAP. Non-F&O stocks keep the VWAP close; F&O trading is extended to 15:40. Source is a broker blog, not a circular. — [INDmoney](https://www.indmoney.com/blog/stocks/stock-market-timings-change-from-3-august-2026-new-cas-timings-explained). Conflicting report: after trader pushback SEBI moved the closing auction to 15:30-15:40. — [NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/sebi-changes-closing-auction-rules-after-trader-pushback/story). Must be verified against NSE circulars before any 15:00-15:30 measure is used after 2026-08-03.
- Using only trade prices understates reaction speed and size. — [Grégoire & Martineau, SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3060094)
- Hou-Moskowitz report their delay results hold using only closing prices. — [Search summary of HM PDF](http://www.ruf.rice.edu/~jgsfss/moskowitz.pdf)

Feasibility notes common to all:
- The market minute return must be built in-house (cap-weighted average of our universe's 1-min returns); no index minute bar is listed in our data.
- Use 5-min or longer windows for any reaction measure: we have no quotes, and Grégoire-Martineau show trade prices understate speed.
- BSE timestamps start in 2018, so E-family backtests have about 8.5 years, not 11.
- Survivors only: E-family and D1 results will be biased upward, as for every minute-bar test.

### Gaps
- No Indian, minute-level, peer-reviewed evidence for any candidate above was found. All India evidence is daily (Prasad & Prabhu; leakage studies) or index-level (Nifty futures-spot).
- The CAS start date and window (15:15-15:35 vs 15:30-15:40) conflict between sources and need an NSE circular.
- The "warp speed" 2026 arXiv paper and the Jiang-Li-Wang full text could not be read, so the strategy magnitudes for news-jump drift are missing.
- Supply-chain lead-lag (Cohen-Frazzini) cannot be tested without customer-supplier links, which we do not have; per the brief, no data purchase is proposed.
- Market-level intraday momentum (first half-hour predicting last half-hour) was not researched; it is a market-timing effect, not cross-sectional, and may be adjacent to the excluded HKS periodicity item.
