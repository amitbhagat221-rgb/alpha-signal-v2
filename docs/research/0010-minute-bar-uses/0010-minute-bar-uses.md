# Research 0010 — Minute bars pay off through events and gates

**Status:** research synthesis, nothing built or backtested · **Written:** 2026-10-16 · **Builds on:** [research 0009](../0009-intraday-factor-menu.md) (its 12 candidates S1–S5, F1–F7 and tools T1–T4 are treated as covered) · **Inputs:** four research notes in [notes/](notes/), plus one in-house verification of the closing auction made on 2026-10-16.

The most valuable things the 11-year minute archive adds beyond research 0009 are **not new price-path moments. They come from using the minute clock around events we already timestamp, and from India-specific exclusion gates.** First, re-anchoring our working `announcement_car` at the filing minute fixes how its daily version mis-dates in-session filings. Second, minute jumps split into news-matched (expected to continue) and no-news (expected to reverse); US high-frequency evidence supports both legs ([Jiang, Li & Wang, JFE 2021](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614); [Xiao 2020](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/jfir.12223)). Third, a surveillance gate: stocks entering India's GSM framework lost about **6.8 % CAR over (−4, +5) days** ([Aggarwal, Bhatia & Zaveri, NSE–NYU](https://archives.nseindia.com/research/content/nse_nyu/NidhiAggarwal_Rules_vs_discretion_Impact_of_trading_rules_on_market.pdf)). Among pure factors, the best new slow candidate is the **day-versus-night beta gap** (US long-short 0.8 %/month raw, [Yang & Yin 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/reveco/v96y2024ipbs1059056024006610.html)), and it can be tested on daily prices today. Three non-factor products earn their keep whatever the factor results: HAR-RV volatility forecasts, minute-bar data-quality checks, and a participation-capped execution simulator. Two facts change the existing menu. Indian overnight returns **do not reverse** ([Nair et al. 2024](https://www.citedrive.com/en/discovery/make-some-noise-tonightovernight-return-as-investor-sentiment-index-indian-evidence)), which undercuts S2's US-style negative sign. And from **2026-08-03, F&O stocks close by auction**, which our archive confirms (RELIANCE has no bars after 15:14). That breaks every closing-window measure for F&O names and opens one new, forward-only candidate. Expect few winners: 96 % of published trading-frictions anomalies fail a t ≥ 1.96 replication hurdle ([Hou, Xue & Zhang](https://www.nber.org/papers/w23394.pdf)), and our own bar is |t| ≥ 2.5 plus the BY-FDR haircut.

## The ranked answer: event timing and gates outrank new moments

Expected value here means roughly three things multiplied together: the chance the item works, how much of the live model it touches, and how cheap it is to build and test. Items that improve something already working, or that protect the SMALL and multibagger books from known losers, rank above new standalone factors, because in-house history is harsh on those: all six daily microstructure proxies were dropped, `max_lottery_21d` came out contrarian, and LARGE has no factor with out-of-sample skill ([research 0009](../0009-intraday-factor-menu.md)). Evidence grades follow one scale throughout. **A** = top journal, post-2010 data. **B** = lower-tier journal, or older or US-only. **C** = working paper or analogy. **D** = our own hypothesis. "In-house" marks a result we produced ourselves.

The ranking has one shape: **five of the top six either use our timestamps or protect and measure books that already exist.** The one pure price-path factor near the top, the beta gap, earns its place because it can be tested on survivorship-free daily data now. Section by section, the rest of this report covers the closing-auction break, the new factor candidates, the gates, the non-factor uses, how Indian evidence changes the existing menu, the regime breaks, the skip list, and the order of work.

## The 2026-08-03 closing auction rewrites the data contract

Our own archive settles a point the sources disagree on. **From 3 August 2026, F&O stocks close through a Closing Auction Session (CAS).** The coordinator checked the Kite archive on 2026-10-16. RELIANCE has no minute bars after 15:14 IST from 2026-08-03 onward; before that date it had bars to 15:29. A non-F&O stock (TV Today) still has bars to 15:29. For F&O stocks after that date, the 15:00–15:15 VWAP equals the 15:00–15:30 VWAP, because no bars exist after 15:14. The official close in our daily price table can differ from the last minute bar. On 2026-08-31, RELIANCE closed officially at 1,277.0 against a last minute bar of 1,294.9, about −1.4 %. The auction price therefore exists **only** in the daily close.

The published mechanics fit this. Continuous trading ends at 15:15, the reference price is the 15:00–15:15 VWAP, the auction band is ±3 % of that reference, and order entry closes at a random moment between 15:28 and 15:30. If no equilibrium price is found, the reference price becomes the close ([Wright Research](https://www.wrightresearch.in/blog/how-does-sebis-closing-auction-session-change-market-close/); timings confirmed by [Angel One](https://www.angelone.in/news/market-updates/sebi-closing-auction-session-from-august-3-2026-new-timings-and-rules-for-f-o-stocks)). Non-F&O stocks keep the 30-minute VWAP close, and no phase-2 date was found. Our data contradicts two secondary reports: one put the VWAP window at 15:10–15:40 ([Arihant Plus](https://www.arihantplus.com/blogs/market-updates/nse-extends-f-and-o-market-close-to-3-40-pm-what-traders-must-know)), and another said the auction had moved to 15:30–15:40 ([NewsBytes](https://www.newsbytesapp.com/news/business/sebi-changes-closing-auction-rules-after-trader-pushback/story)). The auction matters at the scale the model cares about. Under the old VWAP close, the final window drew about **10 % of daily NSE turnover**; early data put the CAS final window at 1.6–2.3 % ([Finshots](https://finshots.in/markets/how-traders-allegedly-gamed-cas/)). On the 31 August MSCI rebalance close, CAS turnover was reported at about ₹39,718 cr, roughly **22 % of that day's cash turnover** ([multibagg.ai](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/msci-aug31-rebalance-nifty-impact-cmteu3l64000830s569rimiyj), an aggregator, so indicative only). That is the same day as the coordinator's RELIANCE example, so the −1.4 % gap is probably a rebalance-day auction move, not a typical one. SEBI has already issued an interim order over alleged gaming of the auction on a 13 August 2026 SENSEX expiry (allegations only; [Finshots](https://finshots.in/markets/how-traders-allegedly-gamed-cas/)).

**Correction to research 0009.** Its data table says NSE's official close equals the 15:00–15:30 VWAP and cites RELIANCE on 2026-10-09 (official 1,170.3, "30-minute" VWAP 1,170.6, "last trade" 1,170.9). That claim holds for all stocks only **before 2026-08-03**. After that date it holds only for non-F&O stocks. The RELIANCE example falls after the auction start: its official close was an auction price, its "30-minute VWAP" was really the 15:00–15:15 reference VWAP, and its "last trade" was the 15:14 bar. The closeness of the three numbers that day was a coincidence, not a confirmation of the rule. Four engineering consequences follow:

- `intraday_daily` needs a point-in-time F&O membership flag per stock-day, derivable from our daily F&O bhavcopy.
- Its `px_last` column must mean "last continuous-session bar": 15:29 for non-F&O stocks and all pre-auction days, 15:14 for F&O stocks after 2026-08-03.
- Its `vwap_close_*` columns must store 15:00–15:15 and 15:15–15:30 separately, so both regimes stay exact roll-ups.
- Every closing-window candidate on the menu (S4 late-volume share, F4 closing pressure, F5 true VWAP deviation) needs a regime split at 2026-08-03 for F&O names, and post-auction F&O stock-days are out of sample for backtests. The rule is about 2.5 months old, which is too short to test.

**The auction move is a new candidate, but only as a forward test.** For F&O stocks after 2026-08-03, define `cas_move = ln(official close ÷ 15:14 bar)` and `cas_dev_ref = ln(official close ÷ VWAP 15:00–15:15)`. The second is bounded by the ±3 % auction band, which gives a free data-quality check that can fail today: any F&O stock-day with |`cas_dev_ref`| clearly above 3 % points to a bad close, a bad minute series or a misread rule. The expected sign of `cas_move` for next-day returns is negative, a grade-D hypothesis. The nearest analogue is the US end-of-day reversal, which is temporary price pressure that undoes itself overnight ([Baltussen, Da & Soebhag 2025](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)). Pre-register it now and evaluate it only on forward data, so a mechanism this new cannot be tuned in sample.

## New factor candidates: the event clock beats the price path

The candidates below are **not** on research 0009's menu. Where one sits next to a menu item, the overlap is named and the redundancy test is set. Every minute-bar backtest is survivor-biased, because Kite holds no delisted stocks. Every E-family test has about 8.5 years, because BSE timestamps start in 2018. Use 5-minute or longer reaction windows: we have trade prices only, and trade prices understate how fast and how far quotes move ([Grégoire & Martineau](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=3060094)).

The table rests on one idea. **India has a lot of in-session corporate disclosure, and our BSE timestamps record it.** In the US, newswire timestamps cost money and almost every firm reports after hours. Two facts sharpen the opportunity. At least **85 % of intraday jumps in DJIA stocks have no identifiable news, while 96 % of overnight jumps do** ([Aït-Sahalia, Li & Li, NBER 2024](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w32746/w32746.pdf)). And news-driven returns drift for days without reversing, while non-news returns reverse ([Jiang, Li & Wang](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614)). For India the reasoning chain is: an in-session filing gets a smaller immediate reaction (US evidence), so more of its information is left for later, so `ann_in_session` should carry more drift. That chain is a hypothesis to test, not a finding. Two diagnostics should be computed but not pre-registered as factors, because they have no evidence of their own. The first is filing-minute "reaction completeness" (return over the first 30 minutes ÷ return through day +1). The second is a distraction count: the number of other BSE filings in the same 30-minute window, a free and point-in-time-safe version of the distraction channel in [Jiang, Li & Wang](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614).

One engineering note for future sessions. **The E-family cannot be served by `intraday_daily`.** Research 0009 designed that table as one row per stock-day of additive sums. Event candidates need minute windows anchored on a filing's own timestamp. That calls for a separate extractor keyed by announcement ID, which needs its own `schema.sql` + `tables.TABLES` entry and a 3-stock smoke test, built after `intraday_daily`.

Over a day, the only published Indian result points to continuation of overreaction. **Fetch the full paper before pre-registering a formula.** Building an approximation and then calling it their factor would spend a hypothesis on the wrong object.

## Gates and event conditioners protect the SMALL and multibagger books

Several of the best-evidenced Indian facts concern who gets restricted, not who outperforms. Their natural job is to **remove names from books**, which is exactly what the multibagger desk's failed daily mania screen tried to do. A gate is pre-registered like a factor: write the rule first, then test whether excluded names underperform within their tier over a fixed horizon. Survivorship bias is worst for these names, because surveillance and operator stocks delist or get suspended more often than most. A gate tested on survivors will therefore **understate** its own value.

The circuit detector (T2 in research 0009) needs more care than that menu assumed, because **a stock's band on a given day is time-varying data**. On the 5 June 2026 list, 42 securities had a 2 % band, 565 had 5 %, 177 had 10 % and 2,288 had 20 % ([TeamLease RegTech summary of NSE Circular 414/2026](https://teamleaseregtech.com/updates/article/56591/nse-revises-price-bands-for-securities-from-june-05-2026/)). Exchanges tighten bands as a surveillance action. F&O stocks have no fixed band: they get a ±10 % operating range that flexes after a cooling-off pause. The flex trigger rose from 25 trades by 5 accounts to 50 trades by 10 accounts and 3 members from 2024-06-03. Flexing became stepped (10 → 15 → 20 → 23 → 26 → 28 → 30 %) from 2024-08-19, and "sliding" from 2024-10-21 ([Zerodha](https://zerodha.com/z-connect/business-updates/explainer-on-new-price-band-rules-for-stocks-trading-in-the-fo-segment); [NSE FAOP/63405](https://nsearchives.nseindia.com/content/circulars/FAOP63405.pdf)). In minute bars, a flex shows up as a flat price at the band for the cooling-off period, then a jump. Bands are price caps, not halts: trading can continue at the band price ([NSE price bands](https://www.nseindia.com/static/products-services/equity-market-price-bands)). Until we harvest the daily band file, the detector has to infer the band from the day's extreme. That is ambiguous when, say, a 5 % stock moves exactly 5 %. Whether NSE publishes a downloadable band history was not established.

The cost line deserves emphasis because it decides a live question. **The single biggest decision the minute archive unlocks is whether the SMALL and multibagger books net out after realistic costs.** The literature says bars can answer it in exactly those names, because close-based estimators are most accurate for wide-spread stocks. The same literature warns that bars overstate costs several-fold in LARGE. The 2024-06-10 tick-size cut will also put a level break into every spread estimate: a 5-paise tick on a ₹20 stock is 25 bps, an illustrative calculation from the rule itself.

A skip list saves hypotheses as well as time. The multiple-testing ledger already holds about 269 hypotheses, which puts the Bonferroni bar near |t| 4.2 (ADR 0043). Every family added here raises that bar for everything else.

## Conclusion

The new notes move where the archive's value lies, away from where research 0009 placed it. That menu looked inside the price path for moments and imbalances, and the in-house record of daily proxies says most of those will fail. The more distinctive asset turns out to be the combination of the minute clock with **India's in-session disclosure and its rule-driven trading restrictions**. US researchers had to buy newswire feeds to separate news jumps from noise. We can do it with BSE filing times we already store. Several Indian surveillance rules leave footprints that only minute volume can see: hourly batch prints, weekly-only trading, flat runs at a band. That favours re-anchoring a factor we already trust and gating out names the exchange itself flags, over hunting another skewness premium.

The closing auction is the warning that comes with the opportunity. A rule that changed 2.5 months ago has already broken one example in our own research doc, and it will silently corrupt every closing-window statistic for F&O names unless the data contract carries the regime. The lesson goes beyond August 2026. Bands, ticks, expiries, block windows and settlement have all changed inside the sample, so **market structure is point-in-time data**, and should be stored and versioned like fundamentals, not assumed as a constant.
