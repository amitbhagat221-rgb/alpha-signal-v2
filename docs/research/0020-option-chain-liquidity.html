What the strangle sells and buys, and how liquid each strike is

NIFTY and SENSEX option chains as Kite quoted them at 15:20 IST on the last entry day of each index (NIFTY 30 Oct 2026, SENSEX 27 Oct 2026), the four strikes the rule picks, and the depth of the market at each one.

Background: reading an option chain

Strike
The index level an option refers to. NIFTY strikes are 50 points apart, SENSEX 100.
Call / put
A call pays if the index ends above its strike at expiry, a put if it ends below. Everything else expires worthless.
Bid / ask
The best price someone will pay for the option / the best price someone will sell it at. We sell at the bid and buy at the ask.
Spread
Ask minus bid, shown here as a share of the mid price. A wide spread means the market is thin: there are few quotes, and getting in or out costs more.
Volume
Units traded that day. Zero means nobody has traded that strike yet; any quote on it is a market-maker's guess.
Open interest (OI)
Units held open by all participants. The deeper the OI, the more a strike is "the market's" rather than one quoter's.
Delta
Roughly the market's odds that the strike is reached by expiry. 0.05 means about 1 in 20.
Lot
The smallest tradeable size: 65 units for NIFTY, 20 for SENSEX. "At 32 lots" means 2,080 NIFTY units or 640 SENSEX units a leg.

What the rule does

Two sessions before expiry, at the close: sell the call and the put whose delta is nearest 0.05 (the two short strikes), and buy a call and a put 1.75% of the index further out (the two wings), taking the nearest strike that has an ask. The short strikes are chosen by their price, so they are always where the market is. The wings are chosen by distance only, so the market there may be thin: that is the liquidity question this page shows.
