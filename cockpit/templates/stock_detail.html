A second, independent lens. The peer-reviewed DLM measure: a Data-Envelopment-Analysis frontier of how much operating profit a firm wrings from its costs + capital vs industry peers (or sector where an industry is too thin), then a Tobit regression removes size and cash-flow — what's left is efficiency attributable to management, not the firm's built-in scale. Read it as a current operating-efficiency rank, not a moat/returns verdict. Two caveats: scale-advantaged blue-chips often land mid-pack (their efficiency is roughly what their size predicts), and firms mid–capacity-expansion (a large asset base ahead of current output — e.g. cement makers building plants) score low even against industry peers — a reinvestment-phase signal, not weak management. Output is profit, not revenue, so fabricated / zero-margin sales can't score high. Diagnostic only, not a model weight.
