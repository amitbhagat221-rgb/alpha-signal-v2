Two views, read them differently.
The money view (tiles, chart, holdings) is one basket of 20 names: in Small caps over five months a random 20-name basket lands anywhere between about −12 % and +16 %, so a few points between two books over this window is luck, not skill. The evidence view on each card judges the score on the whole tier every month (1,500 names, not 20): does a higher score mean a higher return next month, and by how much does the top basket beat the tier on average? What the window says about the model
    How to read the numbers
    Rotation
    A monthly review at which the book traded (a name sold, a name bought, or both). The June start is not counted. Turnover is the value sold over the window as a share of the ₹10 L, so 200 % means the book replaced itself twice.
    STT and all costs
    Securities Transaction Tax is 0.1 % of every buy and every sell on delivery. "All costs" adds the other statutory charges (stamp duty on buys, exchange and SEBI fees, the per-sell DP charge), half the bid-ask spread and square-root price impact at the stock's own 20-day traded value (the cost model calibrated on the account's real fills, plan 0024). The NAV already carries all of it.
    STCG
    Short-term capital gains tax: 20 % plus the 4 % cess on the net gain of names held under 12 months, which is every name here. "Paid" is on gains already realised by sells; "if sold" adds today's unrealised gain. Realised losses offset realised gains; a net loss means no tax.
    Benchmark
    The tier's own NSE price index bought at the June close: NIFTY MIDCAP 150 for Mid books, NIFTY SMALLCAP 250 for Small and multibagger books, half each for the published-picks book. A price index carries no dividends while the books' prices do, so the excess is flattered by roughly one percent a year.
    Rank IC and "top 20 minus the tier"
    Each month, every stock in the tier is scored and then its return to the next month is observed. The rank IC is the rank correlation between the two (0 = the score says nothing; 0.05 a month is a useful score; the t-statistic says how surely it is above zero given how many months there are). "Top N minus the tier" is the return of the N best-scored names that month minus the average of every name in the tier, in percentage points: the basket's edge before costs. This uses every name every month, so it is the measure to trust; one 20-name basket is not.
    Luck percentile
    The book's return compared with thousands of random baskets of the same size drawn from the same tier over the same window. The 50th percentile is an average dart throw; only a book past the 80th or so did something chance rarely does.
    The three models
    Slow learns on 6-month returns and holds at least a year. Fast learns on next month's return and can sell any month. Blend is the mean of the two scores, held at least 6 months. Each month every model picks its factors again using only data from before that month.
    The bar
    The test a factor must pass to get into a model. Today's bar is |t| ≥ 2.5. The multiple-testing bar raises it to allow for how many factors were tried. The long-side bar also requires that the best-ranked fifth of stocks really beat the tier, because a book that only buys cannot earn an edge that lives in the losers.
    Grade
    ADR 0073's scale. B needs t ≥ 1.5, passing a luck test (DSR ≥ 0.90 or PBO ≤ 0.30), ahead in at least 3 of 5 calendar years and from at least 10 of 12 start months, with the worst 12 months no worse than −15 %. A is stricter. C is anything less: research only, the tier stays the index.
    How the three models were chosen
    A walk-forward backtest of four bars for choosing factors, {{ d.selection.window[0]|short_date }} 2022 to {{ d.selection.window[1]|short_date }} 2026, on 20-name books after costs and tax against the tier index. The decision rule was written and committed before any result: one bar per model, the same in both tiers, highest average t, and within 0.25 the stricter bar wins. June 2026 onward took no part in the decision.
